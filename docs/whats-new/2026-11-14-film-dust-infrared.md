### Film dust from the scanner's infrared pass
- A film scanner's RGBI file (VueScan "64-bit RGBI", SilverFast HDRi: RGB plus an infrared pass) opens as an opaque picture with the infrared pass kept beside it. Before, the pass was read as transparency: the photo was see-through and every dust speck a hole.
- Remove ▸ Film Dust reads that pass when the photo has one: dust, hairs and scratches block infrared while the film's dyes pass it, so each mark gets a heal spot (specks, patches) or a heal stroke (hairs, scratches) exactly where it is, whatever the picture shows there. A scan without the pass (a camera scan, an 8-bit TIFF) is searched as before, by the sharp bright marks on smooth areas; `spot.findFilmDust {infrared: only | never}` picks one or the other.
- A **Film dust sensitivity** slider under the button sets how far the search reaches; moving it on a photo that has film-dust spots runs the search again at the new setting, replacing them (your own spots stay).
